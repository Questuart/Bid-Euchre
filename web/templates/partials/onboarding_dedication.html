This game is dedicated to Bud and Barbara.

After a long day turning vines in the field, my grandparents’ dining table had a rhythm to it. Freshly sliced cantaloupe, a couple of Oreos, and a card game that I didn’t fully understand yet but couldn’t get enough of. That game was Bid Euchre, and it belonged to my grandparents, Bud and Barbara.

My family is funny. Sometimes it seems like we know less about each other’s lives than we do about how each other plays cards. So-and-so always bids conservative. So-and-so plays aggressive and then yells at their partner when it goes sideways. Whether it’s Christmas, Thanksgiving, or a funeral, idle chit-chat in the kitchen will eventually be broken up by hooting and hollering from the other room. Somebody just shot the moon and made it.

Even though games can get a bit contentious, Bid Euchre has a special place in my family. Each time we sit down to play, I think we all feel a little closer to Bud and Barbara.

Fair warning to newcomers: this isn’t the Euchre people play as a sideshow to drinking beer. This game requires strategy, deception, and a little bit of moxie. As far as I can tell, this exact ruleset is something only my family plays. There are similar variants out there, but I haven’t found this one in the wild.

What makes it different is the bidding round. That’s the part that separates the veterans from the novices. How you play your cards matters, but honing your bidding skill is what will turn you into a Thanksgiving day legend.

I hope you enjoy the game. Try it out, climb the leaderboard, and leave a comment if you find bugs or anything that feels off.

I built the bots to approximate real gameplay, but they’re not perfect yet. They’ll make bad bids. They’ll misplay a hand. The best way to sharpen them is to put them up against real people, and that’s where you come in. Play the game. Yell at the bot when it messes up, just like you would in a real game.

One thing worth knowing upfront: when you play, you’re helping me collect gameplay data, including hands, bids, plays, and outcomes. The goal is to use that to improve the bots over time, and eventually share some insights that might help us all play a little better.

Have fun. My hope is that this becomes something we can all play to stay connected and share with anyone who’s never had the pleasure of learning this silly, wonderful game.
