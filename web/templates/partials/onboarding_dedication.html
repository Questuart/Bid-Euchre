This game is dedicated to Bud and Barbara.

After a long day turning vines in the field, my grandparents’ dining table had a rhythm to it. Freshly sliced cantaloupe, a couple of Oreos, and a card game that I didn’t fully understand yet but couldn’t get enough of. That game was Bid Euchre, and it belonged to my grandparents, Bud and Barbara.

My family is funny. We know less about each other’s lives than we do about how each other plays cards. So-and-so always bids conservative. So-and-so plays aggressive and then yells at their partner when it goes sideways. Whether it’s Christmas, Thanksgiving, or a funeral, idle kitchen chit-chat will eventually be broken up by hooting and hollering from the other room. Somebody just shot the moon and made it.

I didn’t realize how much this game meant to me until I went home last Thanksgiving. Each time we sit down to play, I think we all feel a little closer to Bud and Barbara.

This isn’t the Euchre people play to pass an afternoon. What makes this version different is the bidding round. That’s the part that separates the veterans from the newcomers we swear we’ll go easy on. As far as I can tell, this exact ruleset is something only my family plays. There are similar variants out there, but I haven’t found this one in the wild. I’ll write up the full rules in a future post.

For now, the game is here. Try it out, climb the leaderboard, and leave a comment if you find bugs or anything that feels off.

The bots are real competition, but they’re not perfect yet. They’ll make bad bids. They’ll misplay a hand. The best way to sharpen them is to put them up against real people, and that’s where you come in. Play the game. Yell at the bot when it messed up, just like you would across the table.

One thing worth knowing upfront: when you play, you’re helping me collect gameplay data, including hands, bids, plays, and outcomes. The goal is to use that to improve the bots over time, and eventually share some insights that might help us all play a little better.

Have fun. My hope is that this becomes something we can all play to stay connected, and something worth sharing with anyone who’s never had the pleasure of learning this silly, wonderful game.
