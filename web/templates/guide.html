How to Play

{# ---- Quick Start ---- #}
Quick Start

🂡 You and your AI partner vs two AI opponents.

✋ Bid how many tricks you think your team can win (or pass).

🎴 Play cards by clicking them — green border = legal plays.

🏆 First team to 52 points wins. Reaching −52 means you lose!

{# ---- The Basics ---- #}

The Basics

Bid Euchre is a 4-player partnership card game. You sit across from your AI partner, and the two AI opponents sit on your left and right.

  • Deck: A 40-card double deck — ranks 10, J, Q, K, A in all four suits, with two copies of each card.
  • Deal: All 40 cards are dealt, so each player gets 10 cards and every hand has 10 tricks.
  • Goal: Win tricks to score points. First team to +52 wins the match; if your score drops to −52, you lose.
{# ---- Bidding ---- #}

Bidding

Each hand starts with an auction. Starting left of the dealer, each player gets one chance to bid or pass.

  • What to bid: Pick a number of tricks (1–10) and a contract type — a trump suit (♠ ♥ ♦ ♣), High (no-trump, Aces high), or Low (no-trump, Tens high).
  • Highest bid wins: The winning bidder’s team declares the contract. Their team must take at least that many tricks.
  • All pass: If nobody bids, the hand is redealt and the dealer rotates.
  • Moon & Loner: Special 10-trick bids. Moon triggers a card exchange with your partner. Loner makes your partner sit out.
{# ---- Card Play ---- #}

Card Play

  • Follow suit: You must play a card matching the suit that was led, if you can.
  • Trump wins: In suit contracts, any trump card beats any non-trump card. Play trump when you can’t follow suit.
  • Bowers: In suit contracts, the Jack of trump (right bower) and the Jack of the same color (left bower) are the two strongest cards. The left bower counts as trump, not its printed suit.
  • Duplicates: Since it’s a double deck, identical cards can appear in the same trick. If they tie for the win, the first one played takes it.
  • High/Low contracts: No trump suit. In High, Aces are highest. In Low, Tens are highest — the rank order is reversed.
{# ---- Scoring ---- #}

Scoring

  • Made bid: The declaring team scores the number of tricks they won.
  • Set (missed bid): The declaring team loses points equal to their bid.
  • Defenders: The defending team always scores the number of tricks they won.
  • Match end: The match ends when a team reaches +52 (win) or drops to −52 (loss). Expect 6–12 hands per match (about 10–20 minutes).

{# ---- Tips & Tricks ---- #}

Tips & Tricks

Count your sure tricks before bidding. Only bid what your team can reliably win.
Lead with strength. Play your strongest suit early to draw out opponents’ cards.
Save your bowers. The Jacks (right and left bower) are the two strongest trump cards — save them for key tricks.
Support your partner. If your partner is winning the trick, play your lowest card to conserve.
Low contracts flip the ranks. Tens are high and Aces are low — adjust your play accordingly.
Don’t overbid. Getting set costs you your entire bid as negative points.
Watch the score bar. If either team is close to 52 (or −52), bid conservatively.
{# ---- Basic Strategies ---- #}

Basic Strategies

  • When to bid suit: You have 3+ trump cards including a bower, or a long strong suit you can force through.
  • When to bid High: You have multiple Aces across different suits — you can win tricks without needing trump.
  • When to bid Low: You have several Tens and low cards — in Low contracts the rank order is reversed.
  • When to pass: Your hand is weak or scattered. Let someone else take the risk.
  • Trump management: Don’t burn all your trump early. Use them strategically to win key tricks or cut opponents’ strong suits.
  • Read the auction: Opponents’ bids tell you about their hands. If an opponent bids high in spades, they likely have strong spades.
  • Partnership play: Your partner’s bid gives you information. If they bid 6 in hearts, support their contract — don’t compete unless you have a clearly stronger hand.
